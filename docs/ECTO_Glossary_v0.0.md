# ECTO Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Assumption sensitivity** | The capacity to notice, identify, and evaluate assumptions that influence how a theory, model, argument, or empirical test works. |
| **Auxiliary assumption** | An additional assumption needed to derive, interpret, or test a theory’s implications but that is not usually one of the theory’s central claims. |
| **Boundary condition** | A condition that specifies when, where, or for whom a theory’s claims are expected to hold, weaken, change, or fail. |
| **Capability** | A broad, transferable capacity that supports effective, adaptive performance across diverse theoretical tasks, domains, and unfamiliar situations. |
| **Capacity** | An ability or disposition of an entity or system to do something or produce an outcome under suitable conditions. |
| **Causal reasoning** | Reasoning about whether and how changes in one entity, process, or condition produce changes in another. |
| **Coherence** | The extent to which the elements of a theory fit together consistently and without contradiction. |
| **Competency** | The integrated ability to combine relevant knowledge, skills, capabilities, judgment, and resources to perform a substantive theoretical function to an adequate standard. |
| **Computational model** | A formal model implemented as an executable algorithm or simulation that specifies how inputs, states, or processes generate outputs over time. |
| **Conceptual precision** | The capacity to formulate and distinguish concepts, claims, and relations clearly enough to avoid important ambiguity or conflation. |
| **Conditional requirement** | A knowledge, skill, or capability requirement that is necessary only under particular circumstances, for particular theoretical tasks, or for particular kinds of theories. |
| **Construct** | A theoretical concept used to represent or refer to a property, state, process, mechanism, or other feature relevant to a theory. |
| **Construct architecture** | The organized set of constructs within a theory or domain, including how they are defined, differentiated, related, grouped, or hierarchically structured. |
| **Core requirement** | A knowledge, skill, or capability requirement that is broadly necessary for competent performance of a particular theoretical function. |
| **Counterfactual expectation** | A claim about what would be expected to occur under an alternative or hypothetical condition, including conditions that did not actually occur. |
| **Counterfactual reasoning** | Reasoning about what would happen, or would have happened, if some condition, event, or causal factor were different. |
| **Cross-cutting requirement** | A knowledge, skill, or capability requirement that supports competent performance across multiple theoretical tasks or competencies rather than belonging to only one. |
| **Dependency** | A relation in which successful performance of one theoretical activity, skill, or competency requires or is supported by another element. |
| **Diagrammatic representation / model** | A representation that uses a diagram to make theoretical entities, variables, processes, or relations explicit, such as a causal diagram or network. |
| **Empirical interface** | The explicit connections between a theory and empirical research, including how theoretical concepts are measured or manipulated, what observations bear on the theory, and what empirical implications follow from it. |
| **Epistemic judgment** | The capacity to evaluate the strength, relevance, limitations, and implications of evidence when forming or revising theoretical claims. |
| **Epistemic status** | The evidential standing of a claim, proposed target, or theoretical element: how strongly it is supported and how confidently it should currently be treated. |
| **Explanandum (plural: explananda)** | Something that a theory is intended to explain. In ECTO, an explanandum may be a phenomenon, capacity, or problem. |
| **Explanatory value** | The extent to which a theory contributes to explaining or understanding its explananda. |
| **Formal representation** | An explicit representation of theoretical content using a rule-governed system such as logic, mathematics, diagrams, algorithms, or another precisely specified notation. |
| **Formalization** | The process of translating theoretical content into a more explicit and rule-governed representation so that assumptions, relations, and implications can be specified and examined more precisely. |
| **Functional explanation** | An explanation that identifies what a capacity, process, structure, or behavior does or what role it plays within a broader system without necessarily specifying the mechanism that produces it. |
| **Hybrid representation / model** | A representation that deliberately combines two or more representational forms, such as verbal propositions, diagrams, equations, or executable simulations. |
| **Indicator** | An observable or measurable feature that provides evidence about a theoretical construct. |
| **Index** | A measure that combines information from multiple indicators according to a specified rule to represent a construct or quantity. |
| **Integrative reasoning** | The capacity to connect and coordinate information, concepts, explanations, or evidence from different sources, domains, or levels of analysis. |
| **Inter-competency dependency** | A dependency in which successful performance of one competency relies on knowledge, skills, outputs, or judgments associated with another competency. |
| **Intervention prediction** | A prediction about what will happen when a variable, process, or condition is deliberately changed. |
| **Knowledge** | The concepts, facts, principles, methods, and informational resources that a theorist must understand, be aware of, or know how to access in order to perform theoretical work. |
| **Mathematical model / representation** | A formal representation that uses mathematical expressions, such as equations, functions, or probability distributions, to specify relations and derive consequences. |
| **Mechanistic explanation** | An explanation that shows how a phenomenon is produced by specifying relevant components, their activities, and how they are organized. |
| **Model** | A deliberately simplified or structured representation of some target, theory, process, or system that can be used for purposes such as explanation, exploration, derivation, comparison, or prediction. |
| **Model family** | A set of related models that share important assumptions or structure while differing in particular specifications, parameterizations, or variants. |
| **Ontology** | An explicit account of the kinds of entities, properties, categories, and relations assumed or distinguished within a domain. |
| **Operationalization** | The specification of concrete procedures or rules by which a theoretical construct is represented, manipulated, measured, or otherwise connected to observable evidence. |
| **Parsimony** | The extent to which a theory accomplishes its explanatory task without introducing more independent constructs, assumptions, or mechanisms than are needed. |
| **Phenomenon** | A relatively stable and general feature of the world that is supported by empirical evidence and can serve as a target of explanation. |
| **Preliminary sketch** | An early and incomplete explanatory account that identifies candidate components, functions, processes, or relations while leaving important details unspecified. |
| **Problem** | An empirical or conceptual issue, difficulty, or question that a theory is intended to address or help resolve. |
| **Process-based explanation** | An explanation that focuses on the sequence or dynamics through which an outcome develops, such as stages, transitions, feedback loops, trajectories, or time-dependent interactions. |
| **Provisional pattern** | An observed pattern that may be meaningful but is not yet sufficiently established to be treated as a robust phenomenon or explanandum. |
| **Representational flexibility** | The capacity to select, interpret, translate between, and combine different forms of theoretical representation when appropriate to the task. |
| **Research programme** | A coordinated set of research activities organized around theoretical questions, assumptions, models, or goals and designed to guide future empirical, formal, or methodological work. |
| **Scope** | The range of explananda, systems, populations, contexts, conditions, or other circumstances to which a theory is intended to apply. |
| **Skill** | A specific, learnable ability to perform a defined cognitive, methodological, representational, communicative, or practical operation relevant to theoretical work. |
| **Speculative target** | A proposed target of explanation whose existence, recurrence, or relevant properties have not yet been sufficiently established. |
| **Systems thinking** | The capacity to reason about systems of interacting components, including organization, feedback, dependencies, dynamics, and relations across levels. |
| **Taxonomy** | A systematic classification of entities or concepts into categories, often but not necessarily organized hierarchically. |
| **Testability** | The extent to which a theory can be connected to observations or interventions that could provide evidence bearing on its claims. |
| **Theory** | A structured set of concepts, assumptions, and claims intended to describe, explain, or organize understanding of one or more explananda. |
| **Theory diagnosis** | The systematic identification of weaknesses, gaps, tensions, ambiguities, or other problems in an existing theory in order to determine what may need clarification, development, or repair. |
| **Theoretical implication** | A claim, expectation, explanation, prediction, or other consequence that follows from a theory together with relevant assumptions. |
| **Uncertainty management** | The capacity to recognize, represent, communicate, and reason appropriately under uncertainty rather than treating tentative claims as settled. |
| **Verbal theory** | A theory expressed primarily in natural language that states relevant constructs, claims, relations, and conditions in verbal form. |
